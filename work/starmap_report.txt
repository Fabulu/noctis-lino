    The catalogue was written by Borland C++ 3.1 on a 387, where every
    intermediate lives in an 80-bit register and only the final store
    rounds to double. Modern double-everywhere arithmetic rounds five
    times instead of once, and disagrees in the last ulp on nearly half
    of them. Under the arithmetic that actually wrote the file, every
    matched star reproduces its stored value exactly - which is a
    stronger statement than the epsilon match, and it also identifies
    which arithmetic produced the file.
    window budget used by the matched stars (1.0 = the full 1e10):
        p50 2.100e-08   p90 1.679e-07   p99 4.712e-07   max 1.397e-06
    NOTE the whole-catalogue worst case is NOT this small. Half an ulp
    of the stored double, scaled by 1e15, is about 0.111*|id|, which is
    38% of the window for WARP DESTINATION 1 (|id| = 4.84e10). The
    matched stars are all near the origin and so all have small |id|;
    the margin at the far end of the catalogue is a factor of ~2.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
what this does and does not prove
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Python and L.in.oleum agreeing is an IMPLEMENTATION cross-check.
    Both run the same algorithm; agreement says the port is faithful
    and nothing more. The evidence that the algorithm is Noctis's is:
      - STARMAP.BIN: 7579 ids charted by players over two decades,
        none of them produced by us. Reproducing them is the claim.
      - the three anchors: constants the author wrote in his own
        source, independent of the catalogue and of any player.
      - the planet-derived parent identities (starmap_planets.py),
        a structural check with no generator involved at all.
      - the negative controls (starmap_all.py), which must collapse
        the match rate on the SAME binary.
